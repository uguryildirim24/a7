# Bupropion, α7 nicotinic signaling, dissociation and seizure susceptibility: two linked hypotheses with preliminary docking evidence

## Abstract

Bupropion inhibits α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptors in experimental preparations, while its metabolites have incompletely characterized α7 pharmacology. We develop two related hypotheses from a focused critical review and an existing stereoisomer-resolved docking study. Hypothesis 1 proposes that, in susceptible individuals with sufficient target exposure, α7 inhibition contributes to bupropion-associated depersonalization or derealization by disturbing interneuron-dependent sensory selection and the integration of bodily and external signals. Hypothesis 2 proposes that a shared loss of cholinergic support for inhibition can produce sensory-processing disturbances at lesser perturbation and increase seizure susceptibility at greater perturbation, provided both outcomes involve the same susceptible circuit. Evidence supports individual links, including parent-drug α7 inhibition, circuit-specific cholinergic recruitment of inhibition, and dissociative experiences during some focal seizures. The complete drug–receptor–circuit–symptom chain remains untested. Null findings, opposite effects of α7 activation, non-α7 pharmacology and depersonalization imaging findings constrain the proposals. Docking identifies candidate metabolite locations but supplies neither potency nor human target engagement. We specify discriminating predictions, conditions under which the hypotheses separate, and observations that would reject each mechanism.

## The two hypotheses

**Hypothesis 1 — an α7 contribution to bupropion-associated dissociation.** In a susceptible subgroup, bupropion—and only those metabolites subsequently shown to inhibit α7 at relevant unbound concentrations—reduces cholinergic recruitment of inhibitory interneurons in sensory and associative circuits. The resulting disturbance of sensory selection contributes to altered integration of external information with bodily and emotional signals, producing depersonalization and/or derealization.

**Hypothesis 2 — a conditional shared mechanism for sensory disturbance and seizure susceptibility.** In circuits where α7 activity normally strengthens effective inhibition, increasing inhibition of that receptor by bupropion or an active metabolite progressively weakens network control. Lesser disturbance can alter sensory processing; greater disturbance can facilitate pathological synchronization and lower seizure threshold. This shared-mechanism hypothesis predicts linked, α7-dependent intermediate changes across the two outcomes. It does not assume that every dissociative experience is epileptic, that symptoms must progress to seizures, or that all bupropion seizures use this mechanism.

These are distinct claims. Hypothesis 1 could hold while Hypothesis 2 fails, for example if dissociative symptoms involve α7 but convulsions arise mainly from another target. A seizure-specific α7 contribution would likewise not establish the dissociation hypothesis.

## 1. Clinical starting point and definitions

The current Wellbutrin XL label lists depersonalization and derealization among postmarketing nervous-system events, while explicitly stating that voluntary reports cannot reliably establish frequency or causality. Its seizure warning provides a separate clinical motivation. A list of reported events does not demonstrate that the events share a receptor mechanism. [1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a435da9d-f6e8-4ddc-897d-8cd2bf777b21)

Here, **depersonalization** denotes subjective detachment from oneself or one's body; **derealization** denotes altered reality or familiarity of the surroundings. Medication-associated symptoms, persistent primary depersonalization–derealization disorder, and brief seizure-associated experiences must be analyzed separately. **Sensory gating** is also not a single endpoint: auditory P50 suppression, visual P1 attention effects and acoustic-startle prepulse inhibition (PPI) measure different processes. None is a substitute for a validated dissociation assessment.

## 2. What is established at the receptor level

Parent bupropion has experimental α7 inhibitory activity. Slemmer et al. compared several nicotinic subtypes; Alkondon and Albuquerque found no inhibition of the α7-like response at 1 µM in their native-slice experiment, despite effects on other response classes. Papke et al. measured human α7 IC₅₀ values of 46 ± 7 µM when bupropion was coapplied with 300 µM acetylcholine and 16 ± 3 µM after 30 minutes of bath exposure probed with 60 µM acetylcholine; those authors described α7 potency as roughly unchanged between protocols, whereas (α4)₂(β₂)₃ and (α4)₂(β₂)₂α5 shifted much more (72 → 4.4 µM and 23 → 2.8 µM). Under bath application all three α4-containing forms were more potent than α7; under coapplication (α4)₂(β₂)₃ was less potent. Vázquez-Gómez et al. reported a 54 µM calcium-response IC₅₀ and native-neuron inhibition; Duarte et al. reported inhibition of rat α7-containing currents at 20 and 50 µM. These results demonstrate assay-dependent inhibition, not therapeutic human receptor occupancy. [2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10991997/), [3](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.104.081232), [4](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.110.177485), [5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2014.06.059), [6](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26040998)

The metabolite evidence requires a different statement. In the directly inspected Lukas et al. functional panel, S,S-hydroxybupropion was more potent than parent at α4β2, while R,R-hydroxybupropion was less potent. The panel did not contain α7. [7](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm1003232)

| Compound | α4β2 IC₅₀, µM | α3β4* IC₅₀, µM | α7 inference |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Bupropion | 12 | 1.8 | Separate parent experiments required |
| S,S-hydroxybupropion | 3.3 | 11 | Not assayed in this panel |
| R,R-hydroxybupropion | 31 | 6.5 | Not assayed in this panel |

Values are central estimates from the same assay; the source reports multiplicative SEM. The asterisk allows additional subunits. Neither “hydroxybupropion is more potent at α7” nor “hydroxybupropion is weaker at α7” follows from this table. Damaj's primary abstract supports stereoselective α4β2 pharmacology; Bondarev's abstract supports tested-metabolite α3β4 functional inhibition. Complete original text for those two papers remained inaccessible in this review, and exact unverified entries were not filled in from secondary citations. [8](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.104.001313), [9](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0014-2999(03)02010-7)

The six core hydroxy-, threo- and erythrohydrobupropion stereoisomers should therefore remain separate experimental candidates. Minor metabolites and conjugates require their own functional and brain-exposure evidence; structural identification in urine does not establish CNS action. Synthetic analogues, deuterated compounds and unresolved glucuronide candidates are not interchangeable with established circulating metabolites.

## 3. Hypothesis 1: from α7 inhibition to dissociative symptoms

### Rationale

Nicotinic receptors can recruit GABAergic inhibition. Human cortical and rat hippocampal recordings show nicotinic control of interneuron output, with either inhibition or disinhibition depending on the connection being activated. Rat basolateral-amygdala recordings show a condition in which α7 activity favors inhibition over excitation. Townsend et al. found increased inhibitory currents and reduced pyramidal firing during α7 agonist application; MLA increased evoked firing. These provide mechanistic plausibility for loss of cholinergic inhibition, but not proof of bupropion acting through that route in a patient. [10](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4328(00)00208-4), [11](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2000.83.5.2682), [12](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.01030.2012), [13](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00243.2016)

Clinical sensory evidence is mixed. The small DMXB-A trial and a tropisetron trial reported P50 improvements in schizophrenia; an α7 positive-allosteric-modulator trial did not. Tropisetron also acts at 5-HT₃ receptors, and results in schizophrenia do not establish effects in depersonalization. A CHRNA7 promoter study found no association of the tested variant with P50 suppression or PPI, despite association with schizophrenia and a startle finding. [14](https://doi.org/10.1001/archpsyc.63.6.630), [15](https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-859X-9-27), [16](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2012.06.040), [17](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12017-015-8371-9)

The most relevant symptom studies support altered processing without identifying α7. Schabinger et al. found reduced early suppression of unattended visual stimuli in depersonalization. Dale et al.'s small depersonalization subgroup broadly resembled controls on PPI, whereas abnormalities concerned a separate dissociative-identity group. Bupropion did not disrupt PPI in Mori et al.'s mouse experiment. These findings require endpoint-specific predictions rather than a blanket claim of failed filtering. [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2018.02.014), [19](https://doi.org/10.2147/ndt.s3006), [20](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2013.07.046)

### The missing bridge and competing explanation

The proposed transition from altered sensory selection to altered self-experience is an inference. Medford et al. associated depersonalization with reduced insula responses to emotional stimuli and a prefrontal pattern consistent with altered regulation; the small treatment follow-up was neither blinded nor placebo controlled. A single-patient interoception study and a later connectivity study implicate bodily-signal and self-referential processing, without measuring α7. [21](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00432), [22](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0098769), [23](https://doi.org/10.1002/brb3.70314)

A local loss of inhibition cannot simply be equated to these fMRI findings. One possible bridge is disrupted weighting of bodily versus external signals, followed by altered network regulation; that additional bridge remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated compensatory response. An equally viable alternative is that anxiety, arousal, sleep disruption, catecholaminergic effects or frontolimbic regulation explain the symptoms without a material α7 contribution. In an eight-week randomised comparison of paroxetine (n = 36) and bupropion (n = 38) in suicidal depression, the HDRS-24 “Disturbed Thinking” factor — lack of insight, depersonalization/derealization, paranoia and obsessions/compulsions — showed no differential treatment effect, reported only as p > 0.1 in a post-hoc analysis with no effect estimate and no item-level result. Because psychosis was excluded at entry, three of that factor's four items would be expected to sit near floor in major depression, so the composite is a weak instrument rather than a test of the individual symptom; bupropion was also dosed at 150 mg daily for the first two weeks, which the trial's authors note many clinicians would not consider a minimally effective dose. [24](https://doi.org/10.4088/JCP.12m08000)

### Predictions and rejection criteria

| Testable prediction | Evidence that would support H1 | Evidence that would weaken or reject the proposed route |
|---|---|---|
| Direct target action precedes the circuit effect | Parent or an identified stereoisomer inhibits human α7 within a justified unbound exposure range | No relevant inhibition; symptoms and circuit effects remain at exposures below the demonstrated range |
| α7 contributes specifically to altered inhibitory recruitment | Drug changes in identified interneuron output diminish when α7 engagement is selectively prevented, with baseline function preserved | Effects persist despite verified loss of the drug's α7 interaction and are explained by matched non-α7 pharmacology |
| The proposed sensory intermediate relates to symptoms | Within-person changes in a prespecified sensory measure track independently measured dissociation after accounting for confounds | Sensory changes occur without symptoms, or symptoms track another mechanism without the predicted intermediate |
| DP and DR are measured separately | Replicable symptom-specific associations with the predicted circuit changes | Only a broad distress or composite score changes |

Failure of one sensory assay rejects that particular predicted intermediate, not every possible α7 effect. Conversely, positive P50 or PPI findings alone would not confirm H1. The clinical arm should be observational during independently indicated care; the hypothesis does not require deliberate symptom induction.

## 4. Hypothesis 2: testing a shared graded mechanism

### Rationale

Several studies support protective cholinergic inhibition in particular seizure circuits. Sun et al. found that reducing α7 activity increased CA1 excitability and shortened pilocarpine seizure latency; their agonist did not significantly improve those outcomes, and the PV interpretation was not established by PV-specific receptor deletion. Wang et al. (2020) used cell-type manipulations to show that a septum–hippocampus cholinergic pathway suppressed mouse seizures through somatostatin-positive interneurons; silencing PV-positive cells did not reverse this protection. A 2025 study linked huperzine-associated seizure protection to septal–dCA1 cholinergic signaling and α7 pharmacology. These are complementary experiments, not one continuous demonstration of a bupropion–PV–α7 mechanism. [25](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.656752), [26](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2019.11.014), [27](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-025-01522-w)

Drug-specific seizure evidence also exists. Silverstone et al. found greater convulsive effects of tested hydroxy, threo and erythro preparations than parent in an acute mouse comparison. The doses were equimolar bupropion-HCl equivalents, individual enantiomers were not separated, and the parent CD₅₀ estimate extended beyond the tested range. The experiment neither identifies α7 nor provides a human metabolite-risk ranking. Tutka et al. found lower-dose protection against electroshock and higher-dose convulsions, showing that even the parent drug's seizure effects depend on the model. [28](https://doi.org/10.1186/1744-859X-7-19), [29](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2004.07.105)

Human overlap between dissociation and seizures provides motivation, not the molecular explanation. Heydrich et al. studied nine patients with ictal depersonalization-like experiences and seven with derealization-like experiences. The former were predominantly associated with frontal seizure networks and the latter with temporal networks. This selected focal-epilepsy cohort does not show that primary DPDR is epileptic or that bupropion symptoms progress along a seizure continuum. [30](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.50870)

### Why a universal continuum is not supported

α7 is an excitatory cation channel expressed in multiple cell types. Its net network effect depends on which cells it excites, their connections and the prevailing state. Gain-of-function α7 mutant mice were more susceptible to nicotine-induced seizures, with MLA suppressing those seizures. In another nicotine model MLA was protective, whereas α7-null mice retained similar nicotine seizure dose-response curves. A separate Chrna7-deficiency study detected no consistent behavioral or EEG phenotype. [31](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-200202110-00004), [32](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2017.00057), [33](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0169-328x(01)00309-6), [34](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep39941)

Other results constrain a simple GABA-depletion account. Cytisine and tropisetron reduced recurrent seizures in rat TLE paradigms with pharmacological evidence implicating α7, but measured bulk GABA did not increase; this does not exclude changed synaptic inhibition, yet it cannot prove that intermediate. Nicotinic blockade also failed to change some hippocampal seizure-like discharges when restricted to α7, while broader mecamylamine blockade was effective. [35](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2021.706225), [36](https://doi.org/10.1111/cns.70086), [37](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0240074)

The shared hypothesis consequently requires three demonstrated conditions: relevant α7 inhibition by the drug, a net loss of effective inhibition in the studied circuit, and an α7-dependent relationship between the proposed sensory disturbance and seizure susceptibility. The **strong graded version** predicts that both outcomes covary with the same intermediate within a matched preparation or longitudinal setting. If these conditions fail, separate mechanisms are the better explanation. Dissociation and convulsive seizure measures in different studies cannot by themselves establish an ordered trajectory.

### Discriminating predictions

| Proposed observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| A drug-induced loss of α7-dependent inhibitory recruitment predicts both sensory disruption and reduced seizure threshold | Supports the shared pathway, subject to exposure and specificity checks |
| A selective intervention preventing the drug–α7 interaction reduces both outcomes without merely suppressing baseline excitability | Stronger evidence for mediation than a general anticonvulsant rescue |
| Sensory effects are α7-dependent, while seizure effects persist after α7-specific protection | H1 remains viable; the shared version of H2 is weakened or rejected |
| Seizure susceptibility changes without the proposed sensory intermediate | Supports a seizure-specific pathway or competing mechanism, not a required sensory-to-seizure progression |
| Different stereoisomers separate the outcomes after controlling for exposure and other targets | Demonstrates mechanistic heterogeneity; tests rather than assumes the metabolite contribution |

An α7 agonist “rescue” alone is insufficient: it could improve network behavior through a parallel pathway, have other targets, or change receptor desensitization. Use convergent pharmacological and cell-specific evidence, controls for the rescue agent alone, and comparison of the drug-induced change relative to each preparation's baseline. Lifelong knockout can alter development, so failure or apparent rescue in that model requires separate interpretation. Animals can test sensory physiology and seizure susceptibility; human subjective depersonalization cannot be inferred from their behavior alone.

## 5. Exposure and alternative targets constrain both hypotheses

Published rat studies distinguish free extracellular exposure from tissue accumulation. Cremers et al. measured microdialysis concentrations and fitted brain/plasma unbound ratios of 1.9 for parent and 1.7 for hydroxybupropion; their human values were predictions. Bhattacharya et al. found time-varying, stereoselective ratios using a different approach. A 2024 rat study estimated a parent whole-brain unbound/plasma ratio of 1.94 ± 0.57 while separately assessing intracellular accumulation. These observations justify studying brain disposition; they do not demonstrate sufficient human α7 inhibition. No new exposure model, dose conversion or occupancy estimate is produced here. [38](https://doi.org/10.1124/dmd.115.068932), [39](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13318-023-00817-9), [40](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncel.2024.1493644)

Competing pharmacology is substantial. Parent and hydroxybupropion affect 5-HT₃ receptors; bupropion also affected α4β2 and potassium channels in a human-channel/network screen. TRESK inhibition was measured separately. Conversely, bupropion was an exception to GIRK inhibition reported for several other antidepressants. Thus even “potassium-channel blockade” requires a named channel and assay. No individual channel hit demonstrates the cause of human seizures. [41](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2016.09.021), [42](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.119.118349), [43](https://doi.org/10.1093/toxsci/kfad087), [44](https://doi.org/10.4196/kjpp.2016.20.4.379), [45](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.npp.1300484)

Both hypotheses are more constrained for routine treatment-associated symptoms than for high-exposure toxicity: the relevant free-concentration premise must be established separately for each setting. Toxicology, therapeutic use and disease-associated circuit alterations are not interchangeable exposure conditions.

## 6. What the completed docking study contributes

The preserved study used human α7 cryo-EM templates 8V82 and 8V8A, four regions per template, three seeds, and Vina 1.2.7 with exhaustiveness 32. Both are epibatidine/PNU-120596 complexes that the depositing authors characterise as desensitized intermediates — "partially activated or partially desensitized" — with the time-resolved 8V8A virtually identical to the equilibrium 8V82; that work did not obtain a fully activated α7 state. These two templates are therefore not two functional states, and both carry a bound type II positive allosteric modulator, which the searches here do not account for. Its 22 species comprised 20 metabolites or structurally qualified candidates and two parent references, represented by 28 forms. There were 672 metabolite/parent calculations plus 12 shared controls. Chemical forms and uncertain glucuronide linkage were explicitly qualified; no physiological protonation fractions were assigned. The full methods and provenance remain in the [saved combined report](/path/to/project/outputs/combined_metabolite_docking_report.md).

In the matched mid-pore searches, R,R-hydroxybupropion produced pore-lumen rank-1 placements in 12/12 runs across two forms and both templates; S,S-hydroxybupropion produced 8/12. Parent rank-1 placements in those boxes landed at the lateral I34 pocket. Earlier parent-only searches also produced pore poses, so the contrast is sensitive to setup. These counts are descriptive computational outcomes, not biological replicates, site preferences, affinity rankings or evidence that R,R is the stronger α7 antagonist.

Three of four ligand/receptor control pairs recovered the deposited geometry at rank 1. I34/8V82 failed at rank 1 (6.372–6.411 Å) but recovered at rank 2 (0.744–0.783 Å). There was no experimental pore-bound blocker control. Thirteen rank-1 poses and 93 saved modes carried the specified short-contact flag. All alternatives and flags remain available; the loss of 12 early interrupted-attempt logs/receipts is disclosed in the included incident record. No settings were retuned to turn the failed control into a success.

The usable structural prediction is therefore: **test whether either hydroxybupropion stereoisomer functionally inhibits human α7 and whether the candidate pore contacts contribute.** Mutational tests would need expression and gating controls because a pore mutation can change drug sensitivity indirectly. Docking adds specificity to the proposed molecular test; it does not verify any circuit or clinical arrow in H1 or H2.

## 7. Evidence assessment and contribution

| Claim | Assessment after review |
|---|---|
| Parent bupropion inhibits α7 in experimental preparations | Directly supported; potency depends on assay |
| Each human metabolite inhibits α7, with hydroxybupropion more potent than parent | Not established in the verified primary records |
| α7 can recruit inhibition and influence seizure susceptibility | Supported in specific circuits; opposite and null results also exist |
| Bupropion causes dissociation through α7 | Hypothesis; target exposure and symptom mediation unverified |
| Dissociative symptoms and seizures share a graded α7 mechanism | Stronger hypothesis requiring joint evidence; not established by separate endpoints |
| Docking demonstrates binding or affinity | Unsupported; the outputs are candidate geometries |

These assessments describe directness and limitations; they are not formal GRADE ratings. The contribution is a pair of explicit causal proposals that can be tested separately and together, with stereoisomers, circuit sign and exposure as discriminating variables. It is not a claim to have discovered nicotinic antagonism, established hydroxybupropion α7 potency, or proven a universal dissociation–epilepsy continuum.

## 8. Review methods, source quality and remaining limits

The evidence table contains **104 endpoint-specific observations from 84 papers**. Consensus supported discovery; Scite supported citation tracing and access to indexed text; PubMed, public primary articles and prescribing information supplied verification. The search was expanded using explicit title/abstract terms to reduce irrelevant automatic term mapping. Six new PubMed queries returned 208 drug–nicotinic records, one metabolite–α7 record, 38 drug–dissociation/gating records, 108 drug–seizure-mechanism records, 191 α7–seizure records and zero α7–depersonalization/derealization records. The single metabolite–α7 hit concerned a behavioral varenicline task, illustrating why hits need screening. The deduplicated deep metadata file contains 528 journal records; it is not 528 full-text reads. The 208 drug–nicotinic titles were screened for direct assay leads, with selected relevant primary records followed in detail.

Scite forward graphs for Damaj 2004, Papke 2011 and Vázquez-Gómez 2014 contained 210, 61 and 22 resolved edges; a Vázquez backward graph added 45 reference edges. These overlapping graph counts are neither independent replications nor complete worldwide citation coverage. Supporting-labeled citations often concerned transporters, metabolism or other nicotinic receptors. Primary text was preferred over OCR snippets; one µM-to-mM extraction error and a conference-page result containing adjacent abstracts were identified. Exact ambiguous conference potencies were not used.

The user-supplied citations were also checked against their actual endpoints. The 2014 brain-injury/amygdala paper reported reduced GABA inhibition alongside **increased** α7 currents, not α7 loss. The young-rat visual-cortex paper emphasized α4β2-related inhibition and other cholinergic effects. The genetic P50 citation did not demonstrate the positive association initially attributed to it. [46](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0102627), [47](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhn258), [17](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12017-015-8371-9)

A retracted 2019 α7/seizure paper (PMID 30946925) was excluded; the distinct 2021 Sun paper was evaluated on its own record and was not counted as replication of the retracted article. A Fragile-X result was identified as a preprint and not used as a central evidentiary foundation. Patent results on deuterated bupropion were kept separate from peer-reviewed evidence and human metabolites. Negative searches do not establish novelty, and similar broad nicotinic explanations have appeared in public discussion.

This is a focused critical narrative review, not a registered systematic review or meta-analysis. Source access ranged from original methods/results to primary abstracts. The inaccessible complete Damaj and Bondarev texts, incomplete worldwide indexing, small or selected clinical samples, cross-species differences and assay heterogeneity limit completeness and causal inference. These limits do not prevent formulation of the hypotheses; they prevent presenting them as demonstrated mechanisms. No new docking, MD, free-energy work, exposure modeling, animal experiment or human challenge was performed for this document.
